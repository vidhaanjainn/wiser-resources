The Math

Why stopping kills your actual return.

It's not just emotional — stopping a SIP during a flat market is mathematically the worst thing you can do. Here's why, broken down.

01
How a SIP actually makes money
Rupee Cost Averaging
1.1
You buy more units when prices are low
A fixed monthly SIP means you buy more units when markets are down or flat, and fewer units when markets are high. This is the entire mechanism behind SIP compounding — it thrives in exactly this environment.
1.2
Flat markets are unit accumulation windows
When the market stays flat for 12–18 months, every SIP instalment buys units at low prices. These are the units that deliver outsized returns when the market eventually rallies — and it always has.
1.3
The rally rewards the units you accumulated, not the ones you missed
When the 19% or 47% return arrives, it applies to every unit you hold. Stop your SIP during the flat phase and you simply own fewer units when the multiplier hits.
02
The timing problem no one can solve
Why you can't time the bottom
2.1
You cannot see the rally coming
The 13 prior instances show the rally arrives suddenly and without warning. The market doesn't announce its recovery. By the time the news confirms it's happening, a significant portion of the return is already gone.
2.2
Re-entering costs you more than staying ever would have
When you exit and then re-enter after seeing the recovery, you're buying at higher prices, potentially paying exit loads or taxes, and resetting your investment timeline. The cost of panic is compounded by the cost of re-entry.
2.3
The data removes the guesswork
Every prior investor who "waited for the right time" to get back in underperformed the investor who just kept the SIP running. Consistency beats timing, every single time the data has been checked.
📌 If your portfolio is flat right now, that is exactly where you want to be — accumulating units at low prices before a historically consistent rally. The discomfort you feel is the price of future returns.
Going Deeper

Four things worth internalising.

The mental models behind staying invested when it's hardest to do so.

📊
DATA DOESN'T CARE ABOUT FEELING
Your gut says the market is broken. The data says it's done this 13 times and recovered every single time. When your emotion and 25 years of evidence disagree, trust the 25 years. The discomfort of holding through a flat period is the mechanism, not the warning.
🔄
BOREDOM IS A FEATURE, NOT A BUG
A flat market feels like nothing is happening. But for a SIP investor, everything is happening — you're buying units at the most favourable prices in the cycle. Boring markets are when SIPs do their best structural work. The excitement comes later, at the rally.
⏳
THE INVESTOR WHO STOPS IS ALWAYS THE SAME PERSON
Across all 13 instances, the investors who exited during the flat period are statistically the same cohort as those who re-entered higher and felt regret. The pattern repeats not because the market is cruel but because emotional decisions are predictably timed badly.
🏗️
LONG-TERM WEALTH IS BUILT IN THE BORING PERIODS
The rally gets the headlines. The flat period builds the foundation. Every unit bought during stagnation is the structural layer that the rally compounds on. Wealth in public markets is almost never built during excitement — it's built during the stretches that make people question whether to stay.
